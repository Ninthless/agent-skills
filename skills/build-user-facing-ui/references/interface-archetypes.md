# Interface Archetypes

Use archetypes to choose priorities and density. They are starting points, not visual themes.

## Operational And Productivity Tools

Examples: admin systems, CRM, ERP, support tools, analytics, editors, consoles, internal operations.

Prioritize:

- Fast scanning, comparison, filtering, editing, and repeated action
- Stable navigation and predictable control placement
- Dense but organized information
- Tables, lists, split views, inspectors, and command surfaces when they fit the work
- Clear status, ownership, timestamps, validation, and bulk operations
- Keyboard efficiency and durable selection state

Avoid:

- Marketing composition inside the working surface
- Oversized headings and excessive whitespace that reduce throughput
- Decorative card grids that fragment related information
- Hidden actions needed repeatedly
- Illustration or atmosphere that competes with data

## Commerce And Catalog Interfaces

Examples: stores, marketplaces, booking, subscriptions, product comparison.

Prioritize:

- Clear product identity, media, price, availability, variants, delivery, and terms
- Inspection, comparison, trust, and decision support
- Persistent cart or selection feedback
- Honest hierarchy between primary purchase action and secondary options
- Error recovery for stock, payment, address, promo, and session problems
- Mobile ergonomics for browsing and checkout

Avoid:

- Atmospheric media that hides the actual product
- Ambiguous pricing or selected variants
- Forcing comparison into unrelated cards
- Long checkout flows without progress or recovery
- Fabricated social proof, ratings, inventory, or urgency

## Brand, Product, Venue, And Portfolio Surfaces

Examples: company sites, product launches, hotels, restaurants, events, personal portfolios.

Prioritize:

- A clear first-viewport signal of the actual brand, product, place, person, or offer
- Strong real or purpose-created imagery when visual inspection matters
- Distinctive composition, typography, language, and pacing
- A coherent story through subsequent sections
- A visible next step and a hint of following content
- Credible proof, work, product detail, or location context

Avoid:

- Generic split-screen template composition
- Tiny brand treatment with generic marketing copy dominating the first viewport
- Darkened, blurred, or abstract images that conceal the subject
- Repeated centered sections or uniform feature-card grids
- Decorative visuals unrelated to the actual offer

## Content And Editorial Products

Examples: news, knowledge bases, documentation, publishing, media libraries.

Prioritize:

- Reading hierarchy, navigation, search, recency, authorship, and provenance
- Comfortable line length, type rhythm, captions, and media relationships
- Clear distinction between primary content, related content, utilities, and promotion
- Save, share, progress, history, or annotation when useful
- Responsive reading without losing structure

Avoid:

- UI chrome that competes with content
- Excessive cards around every article or paragraph
- Decorative typography that reduces reading speed
- Weak link distinction and hidden navigation

## Consumer And Native Applications

Examples: mobile apps, desktop apps, communication, finance, health, travel, personal utilities.

Prioritize:

- Platform conventions, safe areas, native navigation expectations, and input behavior
- Direct access to the main user job
- Clear offline, sync, permission, notification, and account states
- Touch ergonomics, dynamic type, keyboard behavior, and device rotation as applicable
- Progressive onboarding tied to the moment of need

Avoid:

- Porting desktop web composition directly to a small screen
- Custom controls that imitate native controls poorly
- Persistent explanations and onboarding panels after they stop being useful
- Ignoring system back behavior, safe areas, or input methods

## Creative Tools And Editors

Examples: drawing, video, audio, slide, document, 3D, layout, and code editors.

Prioritize:

- The artifact or canvas as the primary visual focus
- Stable tool locations and visible mode or selection state
- Undo, redo, save, history, zoom, and recovery
- Inspectors and toolbars sized predictably
- Shortcuts for frequent work and tooltips for unfamiliar symbols
- No layout shift when selection or tool state changes

Avoid:

- Decorative containers around the canvas
- Text-heavy controls where standard icons are well understood
- Toolbars that resize as labels or state change
- Hiding essential editing state

## Games And Immersive Experiences

Examples: browser games, 2D or 3D scenes, simulations, interactive exhibits.

Prioritize:

- The playfield or scene as the primary experience
- A thematic visual language tied to the game world
- Low persistent HUD coverage and clear critical status
- Contextual prompts, menus, pause states, and input-mode boundaries
- DOM for text-heavy controls and canvas or WebGL for the world when practical
- Legibility over motion and respect for reduced motion

Avoid:

- SaaS dashboards layered over gameplay
- Full-width headers and footers that shrink the scene
- Lore, controls, quests, and settings all expanded at once
- Camera or game input remaining active under menus
- Placeholder art when the game's identity depends on visible assets

## Data Visualization

Examples: dashboards, reports, monitoring, maps, scientific and business visualization.

Prioritize:

- The decision or question the visualization supports
- Appropriate chart form, scale, units, uncertainty, comparison, and annotation
- Accessible color, labels, tables, or alternate representations
- Stable dimensions and responsive behavior
- Clear live, stale, loading, unavailable, and partial-data states

Avoid:

- Decorative charts and fabricated data
- Too many equally weighted metrics
- Encoding meaning only through color
- Animations that obscure comparison
- Shrinking complex charts until labels become unreadable

## Mixed Products

Many products combine archetypes. A travel booking site may blend brand, catalog, commerce, and account management. Assign one primary archetype to each screen or mode so incompatible priorities do not compete.

Keep global identity coherent while allowing density and composition to change with the task.
