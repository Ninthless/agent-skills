# Visual Art Direction

Use this reference when visual quality, identity, atmosphere, or distinctiveness is central. Art direction should produce a product-specific visual system, not a fashionable skin.

## Start From Product Signals

Extract signals before choosing visual treatments:

- User expertise, frequency, urgency, and emotional state
- Primary task, dominant objects, and information shape
- Subject matter, physical environment, culture, and provenance
- Brand attributes that can be observed rather than claimed
- Platform, input method, viewing distance, and available space
- Existing assets, materials, photography, diagrams, data, or product form

Translate each important signal into a visible consequence. A field-operations product may need high contrast and stable region geometry. A museum may derive rhythm from exhibition labels, architecture, and collection photography. A music tool may derive timing, density, and motion from tracks and transport controls.

## Gather And Triangulate References

In concept mode, gather a compact reference set when tools and scope allow:

- Two or three direct or adjacent product references
- One reference from another industry with a similar task or information problem
- One non-UI reference such as editorial design, wayfinding, industrial controls, packaging, architecture, film titles, or physical materials

For each reference, extract only relevant principles:

- Composition and region proportions
- Density and pacing
- Type roles and hierarchy
- Color source and semantic use
- Geometry, borders, elevation, and material
- Image framing and crop behavior
- Motion and transition character

Do not average the references into a generic mood board. Decide which principle belongs to the product and why. Do not copy a distinctive complete composition, brand asset, or proprietary visual identity.

## Write The Visual Thesis

Write one sentence that connects the product to observable choices.

Weak:

`Modern, clean, premium dashboard.`

Useful:

`A compact dispatch surface built from map-grid geometry, high-contrast incident signals, and restrained system typography so operators can scan and act under pressure.`

The thesis should constrain layout, type, color, imagery, geometry, and motion together.

## Establish The Silhouette

Design the large shapes before components:

- Dominant content region
- Navigation and command regions
- Persistent versus temporary areas
- Vertical and horizontal rhythm
- Dense and quiet zones
- Media, canvas, table, map, timeline, or reading column proportions

Inspect the screen at thumbnail scale. The primary region and hierarchy should remain obvious without reading labels. Avoid starting from a universal sidebar, topbar, cards, and metrics skeleton unless the task genuinely requires that structure.

## Direct Typography

- Choose type roles from content and platform needs, not from a default font pairing.
- Define display, section, body, label, metadata, numeric, and code roles only when the product needs them.
- Use weight, size, line height, width, case, and alignment as one hierarchy system.
- Keep dense tools compact and stable; reserve expressive type for surfaces where reading pace and brand allow it.
- Use tabular numerals, monospaced text, condensed faces, serif voices, or variable axes only when they support the content.
- Verify long strings, localization, zoom, and platform text scaling.

## Direct Color

- Derive the palette from brand, subject, assets, environment, or platform when those sources exist.
- Assign semantic roles before decorative accents.
- Use neutrals that support the content rather than defaulting to blue-gray or cream.
- Keep status, selection, warning, success, and destructive colors distinct and accessible.
- Let imagery and data influence surrounding color when the subject should dominate.
- Do not use a gradient merely to signal polish or technology.

## Direct Geometry And Surfaces

- Define edge character, radius scale, border logic, elevation, control silhouette, and region separation as one grammar.
- Match geometry to interaction density and subject matter.
- Use cards only for genuinely discrete repeated objects or framed tools.
- Prefer alignment, dividers, background shifts, or whitespace when they express relationships more clearly than containers.
- Keep interactive dimensions stable across states.

## Direct Imagery And Iconography

- Use real or purpose-created assets when users need to inspect a product, place, person, artifact, environment, or game world.
- Define image purpose, aspect ratio, crop, focal point, caption, fallback, and responsive behavior.
- Prefer one coherent icon family and platform-familiar symbols for common commands.
- Avoid stock-like atmosphere when evidence, detail, or authenticity matters.
- Do not make decorative imagery carry information it cannot communicate accessibly.

## Direct Motion

- Define motion through purpose, duration, easing, distance, and continuity.
- Use motion to reveal hierarchy, causality, spatial relationship, direct manipulation, or reward.
- Keep frequent professional actions immediate.
- Let playful, physical, or cinematic motion appear only when it fits the product and does not delay the task.
- Respect reduced motion and preserve state understanding without animation.

## Define Signature Decisions

Choose at least three visible decisions that make the interface belong to this product. They may involve:

- A product-specific composition
- A distinctive but readable type hierarchy
- A material or subject-derived palette
- A unique treatment of the primary object
- A characteristic image crop or data presentation
- A platform-aware command model
- A motion behavior tied to the product metaphor

Signature decisions must improve recognition, task fit, or atmosphere. Arbitrary novelty does not count.

## Reject Model Defaults

Identify the likely model-default version before implementation. Common defaults include:

- Generic sidebar, topbar, metric cards, and chart grid
- Centered marketing headline followed by three feature cards
- Large-radius floating surfaces on a blue-gray background
- Purple or cyan gradient accents with geometric sans typography
- Sparse operational screens with oversized headings
- Dark atmospheric media that hides the subject
- Pills, badges, and icon tiles without information value

Individual patterns are not banned. Reject combinations that appear without product evidence.

## Art-Direction Review

After rendering, review in this order:

1. Silhouette and dominant region
2. Hierarchy and reading or action path
3. Density and information rhythm
4. Typography roles and line behavior
5. Color roles and contrast
6. Geometry and surface consistency
7. Asset relevance and framing
8. Motion and state continuity
9. Product-specific signature decisions
10. Remaining model-default combinations

Fix structural sameness before polishing shadows, radii, or microspacing.
